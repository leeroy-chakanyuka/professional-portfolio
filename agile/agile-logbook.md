## 1. Evidence and source boundaries

An Agile logbook should show **my actual participation** in daily work and sprint activities, including progress, next steps, blockers and reflections. A teammate's logbook can establish team context, but it cannot substitute for my own account.

## 2. Working-day log template

The table below is deliberately **not backfilled with invented stand-up reports**.

| Date | Work actually completed | Planned next step | Blocker or dependency | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *Add verified date* | *Your actual work* | *Your actual plan* | *Actual impediment or none* | *Card, commit, document or note* |

For a selected significant entry, expand the facts into:

**Work area:** backend API, schema design, business logic, testing or technical review.

**Stand-up update:** what I completed, what I plan next, what is blocked.

**Work completed:** concrete technical action and verifiable outcome.

**Next steps:** next implementation or validation activity.

**Blockers / decisions:** dependencies and how they were addressed.

**Evidence:** links to an authentic taskboard card, commit, ERD, PR or test result.

**Learning:** what changed in my approach and why.

## 3. Retrospective

To complete after reconstructing the individual log:

- What went well in my backend work?
- What delayed progress?
- What technical or communication decision would I change?
- What improvement would be measurable next sprint?

## 4. Personal reflection

<a id="personal-reflection"></a>

**To complete from my own work records:**

- **Situation:** Why was a logbook needed in our project?
- **Task:** What did I personally need to track or communicate?
- **Action:** How did I document and respond to real work, blockers and feedback?
- **Result:** What did the record reveal about my progress and improvement?
